# 1) Major Updates vs. the Current Live Site

The current site (yobowlcarrollton.com) is a standard Menufy/HungerRush ordering template. Its homepage is essentially a logo, an "Order" button, an ingredients blurb, an Instagram strip, and reviews — with an **empty "Grid Photo Gallery" placeholder** and a **spelling error ("DELISHIOUS")** still live on the page. The new site rebuilds the whole experience. Here is what changed, area by area.

### A. Brand & Homepage
| Current site | New site |
|---|---|
| Logo only, no headline; clicking the logo sends you to the ordering app | Clear headline **"Authentic Chinese Comfort Food"** + tagline, with the logo linking back home as expected |
| Generic template look | Custom visual identity — branded "YB" mark, warm appetizing color palette, professional typography (Montserrat + Open Sans) |
| Empty "Grid Photo Gallery" heading with nothing under it | Removed; replaced with a real, working Gallery page |
| Typo "DELISHIOUS" live on the page | Fixed and copy professionally rewritten |
| One "Start Your Order" button | Clear, repeated **Order Online** calls-to-action plus a cart indicator in the header |

### B. New & Rebuilt Pages
- **Menu page (brand new).** The current site has **no menu page** on the marketing site at all — visitors must jump into the ordering app to see what's served. The new site has a dedicated, shareable Menu page showing the full menu and the new "Yo! Bowl Snacks" lineup.
- **Catering page (rebuilt).** A proper catering hero, a description, the catering menu, and a clear "Contact Us" path for event inquiries.
- **Location & Hours + Contact (rebuilt).** Now includes the address, click-to-call phone, **business hours AND separate carryout hours**, cuisine & atmosphere tags, an embedded Google Map, a **"where to park" storefront guide photo**, and a working contact form.
- **Gallery (rebuilt & functional).** A real photo gallery with a private, password-protected upload tool so you can add dish photos yourself — no developer needed for day-to-day updates.

### C. SEO & Discoverability (mostly invisible, very important)
- **Unique page titles & descriptions** on every page (Home, Menu, Gallery, Location, Catering) — the building blocks Google shows in search results. The current template uses one generic description sitewide.
- **Google "rich results" data (Schema.org structured data):** the site now formally tells Google it's a **Restaurant** — with address, phone, hours, price range, cuisines, geo-coordinates, and a structured **Menu**. This is what powers enhanced restaurant listings.
- **Social share previews (Open Graph + Twitter cards):** when the site is shared on Facebook, iMessage, or X, it now shows a proper image, title, and description instead of a blank link.
- **Canonical URLs** on each page to avoid duplicate-content confusion.

### D. Performance, Accessibility & Mobile
- **Faster loading:** image preloading/preconnect for the hero, lazy-loading for below-the-fold images, and **optimized, web-sized photos**.
- **Gallery speed fix:** the Gallery's dish photos were originally uploaded as full-resolution PNGs (4–5 MB each, ~13 MB for the page). They were converted to optimized JPEGs at the same resolution, cutting the page's image payload from **~13 MB to ~1.2 MB (about 10× faster)** with no visible quality loss.
- **Mobile-first responsive layout** with a proper hamburger menu.
- **Accessibility & SEO basics:** descriptive `alt` text on images, labeled buttons, and semantic headings.

> **Bottom line:** the current site is a functional ordering link with a thin storefront. The new site is a complete, fast, search-optimized restaurant website that still drives every visitor toward placing an order.

# 2) Top 5 Future Improvements (Conversion Rate + SEO)

Ranked by impact-to-effort. These are the next things worth doing.

### 1. Replace stock/menu-photo imagery with real, high-quality dish photography
Appetizing, authentic photos of the actual Braised Beef Mifen, dumplings, and yogurt are the single biggest driver of food-ordering conversions. **Why it helps:** photos sell the food and reduce hesitation; fresh original images also give Google unique content to index and feed the Google Business Profile. **Effort:** low–medium (one good photo shoot).

### 2. Add ordering deep-links and a sticky "Order Online" button on mobile
Link "Order Online" buttons (and individual dishes, where possible) directly to the relevant item in the ordering app, and keep a persistent order button visible as users scroll on phones. **Why it helps:** every extra click loses customers; shortening the path from "interested" to "in cart" directly lifts conversion.

### 3. Capture reviews and show a live rating + review count
Add a "Leave us a review" flow (QR code in-store, follow-up text/receipt link) and display your live Google star rating on the site. **Why it helps:** review quantity/recency is a **major local-ranking factor**, and visible social proof increases trust and order rate. The site already has a review section ready to feature them.

### 4. Add a few SEO landing sections/pages around how people actually search
Short, genuinely useful content targeting real queries: "Chinese food near me in Carrollton," "Best rice noodles (mifen) in Carrollton," catering FAQs, and a dish glossary explaining *mifen*. **Why it helps:** these pages rank for long-tail local searches the homepage can't, and answer the questions customers actually type.

### 5. Add analytics, a fast-loading optimization pass, and conversion tracking
Install Google Analytics 4 + Google Search Console, track "Order Online" clicks as conversions, and run a Core Web Vitals/PageSpeed pass. **Why it helps:** you can't improve what you don't measure — this tells you which pages and buttons actually drive orders, and page speed is a confirmed ranking and conversion factor.

*(Bonus / quick wins: add an FAQ section with FAQ schema, a newsletter/SMS sign-up for repeat orders, online-ordering for delivery if not already offered, and seasonal/specials banners.)*

# 3) SEO Advice & Site Analysis

### A. On-site SEO — what's already done vs. what to add

**Already in place on the new site:**
- Unique titles & meta descriptions per page
- Restaurant + Menu structured data (Schema.org)
- Open Graph / Twitter social previews
- Canonical URLs, semantic headings, image alt text
- Fast images (optimized/right-sized JPEGs, lazy-load, preload/preconnect) — including a Gallery fix that cut its image payload ~10× (oversized PNGs → optimized JPEGs)

**Recommended next steps (technical SEO checklist):**
1. **Submit an XML sitemap and `robots.txt`** to Google Search Console so every page gets crawled.
2. **Verify the site in Google Search Console & Bing Webmaster Tools** to monitor indexing, clicks, and errors.
3. **Ensure NAP consistency** — the exact same **N**ame, **A**ddress, **P**hone on the website, Google Business Profile, Yelp, Facebook, and every directory. Inconsistencies hurt local ranking.
4. **Add `AggregateRating` and `Review` schema** once reviews are syndicated, to enable star ratings in search.
5. **Add an FAQ page with FAQPage schema** (parking, hours, spice levels, catering minimums).
6. **Build local citations & backlinks** — Yelp, TripAdvisor, local Carrollton/DFW food blogs, the Metrocrest Chamber, food-delivery listings.
7. **Run Core Web Vitals / PageSpeed Insights** and fix anything flagged (image sizing, render-blocking resources).
8. **Set up GA4 + click tracking** on all "Order Online" buttons.

### B. Google Business Profile (GBP) — Top 10 Things to Improve

> **Note:** I can't log into your Google Business Profile from here, so I can't read your live dashboard. The list below is the **standard high-impact GBP audit** for a local restaurant — work through it inside your Google Business Profile manager (business.google.com), and most of these will likely apply. For a true live audit, share a screenshot or read-only access to the profile and I'll mark off exactly what's done.

1. **Claim & verify the profile** (if not already), and make sure **you** control it — not a former developer or a third party.
2. **Pick the right primary category** ("Chinese Restaurant") plus accurate secondary categories ("Noodle Shop," "Asian Restaurant," "Takeout Restaurant"). Category is one of the strongest local-ranking levers.
3. **Confirm NAP + hours are exact and current**, including holiday hours and your separate **carryout hours** — and that they match the website exactly.
4. **Add the website + a direct "Order Online" / menu link** so the listing routes traffic to ordering, and add the **Menu** link.
5. **Upload lots of high-quality photos** — exterior (so people recognize it), interior, food, and team. Profiles with more photos get significantly more clicks and direction requests. Refresh monthly.
6. **Actively gather reviews and reply to every one** (good and bad). Volume, recency, and owner responses all influence ranking and trust.
7. **Post regularly with Google Posts** — specials, new "Yo! Bowl Snacks," catering offers, events. Posts signal an active business.
8. **Fill in all attributes & services** — dine-in / takeout / delivery, "good for groups," accessibility, payment methods, "serves vegetarian," etc.
9. **Use the Q&A section proactively** — seed and answer common questions (parking, spice levels, catering, reservations) before customers do.
10. **Add products/menu items with photos & prices** inside GBP and keep the description keyword-relevant ("authentic Chinese mifen and dumplings in Carrollton, TX"). Then **monitor GBP Insights** to see how people find and act on the listing.
